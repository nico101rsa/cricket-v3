Joker balance — how they work & where they landed

Cricket Sim · balance harness (sweep over 2000 even-★3 matches per joker) · 2026-06-10

1 · How a joker works

Every ball, the sim makes two hidden rolls: “did a wicket fall?” and “how many runs?”. A joker is just a multiplier on one of those rolls — nothing more exotic.

🎯 The wicket roll

A joker with wicket ×0.62 (batting) shrinks your chance of getting out. Bowling, wicket ×1.45 grows the chance of taking one.

🏏 The runs roll

runs ×1.40 (batting) makes you score faster. Bowling, runs ×0.62 chokes the runs you concede.

Three things shape a joker:

  • Rarity sets how strong it’s allowed to be — a Common is a small edge, a Legendary is build-defining.
  • A condition (optional): some only fire in a situation — while chasing, after taking a wicket, on a Defensive over. These do nothing until that situation happens.
  • Combos: some jokers (“enablers”) do nothing alone — they switch another joker on. They’re keys, not payoffs.

We measure a joker’s strength as its win-rate lift: how much more often you win with it vs without, in an otherwise even match. That number must land inside its rarity’s band.

3 · Conditional jokers — measured in their situation

These read near-zero above because their trigger doesn’t happen in a neutral match. That’s not weakness — it’s a sleeping joker. Measured in the situation that fires it (or after a mechanic fix), they reach their band. Faded bar = neutral reading; solid bar = in-context / fixed.

The remaining conditional jokers (Strike Bowler, Comeback Press, The Trap, Pace Pack, the bowling-change & Form-combo set…) work the same way — they fire on a bowling change, a 3rd Boost, a catching field, etc. The scenario sweep confirmed they’re dormant-not-weak; each carries a fire-rate used to price it.

4 · What the sweep concluded

The pool is balanced by rarity, and the dangerous outliers are gone.

  • Every always-on joker sits in its band. No joker is negative, none is an auto-win. (The old +28% Review Master is now +9.6%.)
  • “Sleeping” jokers were the trap. ~18 conditional jokers read +0–1% in a plain match — not because they’re weak, but because their situation never came up. Measuring them in situation proved it (The Chase Master: +3% → +13% when actually chasing).
  • You can’t always fix a weak situational joker with a bigger number. The Chase Master’s extra runs are wasted once you pass the target; Wicket Maiden’s extra wicket-chance only pays if a wicket falls. The fix was a second effect that always converts — composure (survival) for the chase, economy (dot pressure) for the wicket over. Both then reached their band.
  • One thing left for the economy rung: a loadout cap. Stacking every joker of one type reads very high (all 8 review jokers = +34%), but you’ll only ever hold ~5 — so that’s a shop rule, not a balance bug.
