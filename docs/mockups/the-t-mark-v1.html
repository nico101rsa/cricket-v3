A T struck with a bail, sitting head-on to a wicket.

Concept · A capital T crossed once below the cap, in the lineage of $, ₹, ¥, ₿, ₱. The strike reads twice: as a currency mark on the stem, and as a cricket bail resting on a stump. T for ton, wicket for cricket.

Form · Geometric sans, monoline, single colour. No serifs that vanish at small size. Sits on the baseline aligned with tabular figures, like $ does.

One bail, or two?

The crucial decision
A · One Bail
  • Cleanest at 16px. One strike survives any rendering, every device.
  • Most "currency". Closest to $, ¥, ₹ — joins the lineage without effort.
  • Wicket reading is softer — emerges on a second glance, doesn't shout.
Pixel cost at 16px: 3 horizontals · top, strike, baseline
B · Two Bails ★
  • Unmistakably a wicket. Two bails on a stump is the head-on view, broadcast-graphic.
  • Echoes the real ₸ glyph (Kazakh tenge — T with double strike). Joins lineage AND has documented heritage.
  • Adds character at display sizes without breaking down at small size if strikes are spaced well.
Pixel cost at 16px: 4 horizontals · needs 2px gap to stay legible

What this is. A wordmark/glyph for the game's score "currency." Designed to function both as a logo lockup and as an inline character next to tabular numerals — the same role $ plays in a price tag.
Why ₸. The Kazakh tenge is the only major currency that already uses a T+strike. We're not the first to think it; we're joining a tiny, well-formed lineage. The cricket reading is the gift on top.
What's next. Lock 04B (two-bail). Cut a single-bail fallback for sub-14px. Test against the locked card-portrait headers and scoreboard chips in In-Match Hi-Fi v2 portraits.html.
Out of scope here. Wordmark + this glyph as a lockup. Animated reveal. Embossed banknote treatment. Engraved trophy plate. All possible — flag the ones you want next round.
