The dots are individual matches (win = top band, loss = bottom band, jittered so you can see density). The gold curve is the win-rate — and its error bars are tiny because each point pools thousands of matches, so the average is pinned even though any single match near the middle is a coin-flip.

Standard deviation

Two different "spreads", don't confuse them:

1. Spread of one match's result (win=1 / loss=0): SD = √(p·(1−p)). It is largest at an even matchup (~0.50 at gap 0 — maximum unpredictability) and falls to 0 at lopsided gaps where the result is a foregone conclusion. This is what the scattered dots show.

2. Spread of the win-rate estimate (the curve's wobble): SE = SD/√matches ≈ 0.009 at gap 0 — i.e. the measured 52.6% is accurate to ±~1%. That's why the curve is smooth.

Bonus — score spread: the Player team's innings total averages ~109 ± 37 runs at an even matchup. (At big favourable gaps the score SD grows somewhat — when crushing a weak side you sometimes chase a tiny target and stop early at a low total, so totals skew. Not a bug; just chase truncation.)
